Signs You Are Under Magical Attack — and What to Do First

Unexplained fatigue, sudden financial collapse, recurring nightmares, blocked relationships. A diagnostic guide before any ritual begins.

✦ Shamman Ondieki Master of Black Magic & Witchcraft

Most people who contact me believe they are under magical attack. Perhaps one in five actually is. The other four are experiencing ordinary life — collapse of a business, depression, bad luck, the natural consequences of their own choices — and are looking for a name to put on their pain.

That is not a criticism. It is the reason this guide exists. Before any ritual is performed, before any fee is agreed, the first step in my practice is always the same: diagnosis. If I diagnose you wrongly, I will treat you wrongly — and you will pay for a cure you did not need while the real cause continues its work.

What follows is the diagnostic framework I have refined over twenty years. Read it honestly. Answer it for yourself. Then, if the signs match, write to me with the details requested at the end.

The Three Categories

Magical attack, when it is real, falls into one of three categories. Each has a distinct signature.

  1. Directed attack. A specific person has paid, asked, or personally performed work against you. This is the most common form of genuine attack and leaves the clearest traces.
  2. Inherited burden. A tie or curse carried through your family line. It does not target you personally — it targets the bloodline. Symptoms usually appear at predictable life stages.
  3. Self-generated obstruction. Not an attack at all, but the accumulated weight of your own choices, words, and agreements. It mimics attack and is often mistaken for it.

The distinction matters because the treatment is entirely different. A directed attack is removed. An inherited burden is severed. Self- generated obstruction is corrected through discipline, not ritual.

Physical Signs

The body is usually the first witness. When magical pressure is applied, it shows physically before it shows in circumstance:

  • Waking fatigue that sleep does not fix. You sleep eight hours and rise exhausted, as if you did not rest at all.
  • Unexplained chest pressure or difficulty breathing in specific rooms or around specific people — with no medical cause.
  • Recurring dreams of the same person, the same room, the same snake, or the same fall. Especially if the dream began abruptly and repeats for weeks.
  • Sudden loss of appetite or compulsive over-eating without lifestyle change.
  • A sensation of being watched when alone, especially at night, especially in your own home.
  • Physical marks that appear and vanish — scratches, small bruises, red spots — usually on waking.

One of these is not attack. Three or more, appearing within weeks of each other, warrants serious attention.

Circumstantial Signs

When magical pressure reaches your circumstances, the pattern is usually too clean to be coincidence:

  • Sudden financial collapse with no clear cause — contracts falling through, payments delayed, unexpected losses arriving one after another.
  • Loss of relationships that were stable — friends, family, or partners turning against you without provocation.
  • Repeated failure at the threshold of success. A promotion is withdrawn. A deal fails on signing day. A relationship ends the week before a wedding.
  • Objects going missing. Keys, rings, documents, photographs — always items connected to identity.
  • Broken electronics, especially phones. Repeated screen cracks, battery failures, or device loss in a short window.
  • Animals reacting strangely around you — dogs growling, cats refusing to enter a room, birds staying away.

The Three Questions I Ask Every Client

Before I will accept a case as magical attack, I ask three questions. If the answer to all three is yes, the diagnosis is almost always confirmed.

  1. When did this begin? Not "years ago," but a specific month. If you can name the month, something changed in that month. Magical work leaves temporal fingerprints.
  2. Who entered or left your life around that time? Attack almost always follows a person — someone new, someone angered, someone you refused.
  3. Is the pattern absurd? Real attack is disproportionate. You do not merely have bad luck — you have luck that seems coordinated, that follows you into every attempt to escape.

What to Do First

If, reading the above, you recognize yourself, do these things before you write to me or anyone else:

1. Rule out the ordinary. See a doctor for the physical symptoms. See a therapist if the circumstantial weight has grown heavy. Magic does not exclude the mundane — it often hides within it.

2. Write a timeline. When did each symptom begin? Who entered your life in that period? What changed in your home, your work, or your relationships in the same month?

3. Stop talking about it publicly. If you are under attack, discussing it openly is the worst thing you can do. The work gains strength from being named. Keep it silent until the diagnosis is complete.

4. Break contact with the suspected source. If you know who is doing this, stop all contact — calls, messages, visits, gifts, gifts received. A severed channel is harder to work through.

What Not to Do

Equally important:

  • Do not buy protection items online. Salt, crystals, amulets sold on marketplaces are inert. They will not protect you. Some make it worse.
  • Do not confront the suspected attacker. If you are wrong, you have done harm. If you are right, you have given them knowledge of your awareness — and time to adapt.
  • Do not perform rituals from the internet. A ritual performed incorrectly at the wrong hour by the wrong person is not neutral. It can open what it was meant to close.
  • Do not test the attacker by "seeing if it works." There is no such test. The pattern is the test.

When to Contact Me

Contact me when you have done the work above and still believe you are under attack. Specifically:

  • You have three or more physical signs appearing in a short window
  • The circumstantial pattern is coordinated, not random
  • You can name the month the attack began
  • You know, or strongly suspect, who is behind it
  • You have ruled out the ordinary causes with a doctor or therapist

Send me your full name, a clear photograph, the timeline you have written, and the name of the person you suspect. I will study your case for two to three days before responding with a diagnosis, timeframe, and price.

If your case is not attack, I will tell you so — and I will not charge you for a ritual you do not need.
