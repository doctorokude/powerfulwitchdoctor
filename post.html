The Dark Baptism: What It Is and When It Is Used

A rite of severance and rebirth — not for the curious, but for those whose path requires a total break from what came before. An explanation for serious inquirers.

✦ Shamman Ondieki Master of Black Magic & Witchcraft

Every practitioner has rituals they speak of publicly and rituals they do not. The Dark Baptism belongs to the second category — not because it is forbidden, but because it is final. Once performed, there is no undoing it. Once performed, the person who entered is not the person who leaves.

Over two decades, I have performed this rite fewer times than I have fingers on one hand. It is not a service I offer casually, and it is not a service I recommend to most who ask. What follows is an explanation — not an invitation.

What It Is

The Dark Baptism is a ritual of severance and rebirth. It formally and completely severs the practitioner's connection to a prior state — a name, a lineage, an identity, an obligation, or a spiritual tie — and establishes a new one under a consciously chosen authority.

It is not a "cleansing." It is not a "protection." It is not a spell for love, money, or luck. It operates on a different level entirely, at the root of identity itself. Those who confuse it with a simple banishing ritual misunderstand both the work and themselves.

A banishing removes a presence.
A binding constrains a presence.
The Dark Baptism removes you from the equation entirely — and writes a new one.

When It Is Used

In my practice, I consider the Dark Baptism only when all of the following conditions are met:

  1. The prior tie cannot be dissolved by any lesser means. Cleansings, protections, and cuttings have already been attempted, correctly, and failed — or are structurally impossible.
  2. The client's life depends on the break. Not comfort. Not preference. Life — safety, sanity, or survival.
  3. The client fully understands what is being severed. There is no "we'll see how it goes." This is not a trial. This is the last door.
  4. The client has accepted that the cost is not only ritual. The Dark Baptism changes the terms of a life. Relationships, opportunities, and even physical circumstances may reorder around it.

What the Rite Involves

I will not publish the ritual here — that would serve no one and harm many. What I can describe is the shape of the work:

  • Preparation of the practitioner and the space over 3–7 days
  • A formal renunciation of the prior tie, spoken aloud
  • The severance itself — performed at a specific hour, under specific conditions
  • The sealing — the establishment of the new authority under which the client will operate
  • A period of integration, typically 3–7 days, during which the client may feel disoriented or dream heavily

The client does not need to be present for all parts of the rite. In most cases, they are not. The work is done on their behalf, with their clear consent, using their photograph and name.

What It Is Not

There are many misconceptions I hear. Let me address the most common:

"It's a pact with a demon." No. The Dark Baptism does not involve pacts with any external entity. It is a restructuring of the client's own position within the unseen — nothing more, nothing less.

"It gives you power." No. It removes obstructions and reorders ties. Any "power" that follows is the natural consequence of no longer being bound — not a gift from the rite.

"Anyone can do it, you just need the words." No. The words are the least of it. What matters is the practitioner's authority, precision, and capacity to hold the space through the entire work. I have seen this rite attempted by others and fail catastrophically.

Signs It May Be Right For You

If you are reading this and wondering whether the Dark Baptism is what your situation requires, consider these signs:

  • You have tried every reasonable method to break free of a bond, obligation, or pattern — and each attempt has failed or been reversed
  • The bond, obligation, or pattern is actively endangering your life, health, or sanity
  • The tie is not to a person, but to something deeper — a name, a bloodline, a debt, an agreement you never consciously made
  • You are not afraid of the consequences of severance, only of remaining bound

If these do not describe your situation, do not inquire about this rite. Ask me about protection, cutting, cleansing, or binding instead. Those are the right tools for almost every case.

How to Inquire

If after reading this you believe the Dark Baptism is what your case requires, send me:

  • Your full name and photograph
  • A written account of what you are bound to, and by what
  • A history of what you have already tried, in detail
  • A clear statement of what you are prepared to have severed

I will review your case for several days. Most inquiries will receive a recommendation to pursue a different work. A very small number will be accepted for the Dark Baptism itself. In all cases, you will receive a direct, honest answer.

— Shamman Ondieki
